Four Oaks grew up after 1880, when Four Oaks Park was bought to become a racecourse. The land was later sold off for building, and it became one of the most sought-after addresses in the Midlands. Many of the houses are large, individually designed period homes, and their roofs need a roofer who's comfortable with traditional materials.

## Typical roofing work in Four Oaks

- **Clay tile repairs and re-roofs**: matching the original handmade or machine-made clay tiles
- **Lead valleys and flashings**: complex roofs have long valleys and lots of junctions, and the lead eventually splits or lifts
- **Chimney stacks**: repointing, rebuilding and new flashings on tall, exposed stacks
- **Dormers and bay roofs**: small lead or flat roofs that are prone to leaks
- **Guttering**: tree-lined roads mean blocked gutters and valleys every autumn

## The conservation area and your roof

Part of Four Oaks has been a **conservation area since 1986**. That's designed to protect the area's character, so the council takes an interest in how roofs look.

As a general guide:

- **Usually fine:** repairs and like-for-like replacement using matching tiles, slates and details
- **Check first:** changing roof materials, adding roof lights, altering dormers or chimneys, solar panels on roof slopes facing the road
- **Listed buildings:** almost any change to the roof needs listed building consent

Rules vary street by street, so check with Birmingham City Council's planning team before making changes. A roofer who has worked in the area before will usually know what's likely to be acceptable.

## Costs

Re-roofing a large Four Oaks house generally costs well above the city average, because of the roof area, complexity and materials. As a starting point, a three-bed semi in clay tiles is typically £9,500 to £15,500, and big detached houses can cost several times that. The only way to get a real figure is a survey.
