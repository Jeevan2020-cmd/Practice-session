Royal Sutton Coldfield has a real mix of housing, and that shapes the roofing work people need. Here's what we see most often across the town.

## Roofs by neighbourhood

**Four Oaks and Little Sutton.** Large detached houses, many from the late Victorian, Edwardian and interwar years, with big, complex roofs: hips, valleys, dormers and tall chimneys, often in clay tiles. Lead valleys, ridge work and chimney repairs are common jobs. Part of Four Oaks is a conservation area. See our [Four Oaks roofing page](/areas/four-oaks/).

**Boldmere and Wylde Green.** Much of the housing here was built in the 1930s, with more in the late 1940s and 1950s. Many of the original 1930s roofs are now around 90 years old. Typical work includes full re-roofs, replacing worn ridge and hip tiles, and repairing the small felt or lead roofs over bay windows.

**Walmley, Falcon Lodge, Mere Green and Minworth.** Mostly post-war and later semis, detached houses and bungalows, often with concrete interlocking tiles. Common jobs are flat roofs on garages and extensions, fascias and guttering, and repairs after storms.

**Town centre and Maney.** Older terraces and Victorian villas, plus flats and commercial buildings. The High Street conservation area covers part of the town centre.

## Conservation areas: check before you change anything

Birmingham City Council lists three conservation areas in Sutton Coldfield:

- **Four Oaks**
- **High Street**
- **Anchorage Road**

Like-for-like repairs are usually fine. But if you want to change the look of the roof, for example swapping clay tiles for concrete, adding roof lights or altering a dormer, check with the council's planning team first. Some streets also have "Article 4 directions", which remove normal permitted development rights. A local roofer who knows the area will know what's likely to be acceptable.

## Leafy roads mean busy gutters

Sutton Coldfield is one of the greenest parts of Birmingham, with Sutton Park and plenty of mature trees on residential roads. That's lovely, until autumn. Leaves, seeds and moss block gutters and valleys, and water then overflows down the walls or backs up under the tiles. A gutter clear-out once or twice a year is one of the cheapest ways to avoid damp problems.
