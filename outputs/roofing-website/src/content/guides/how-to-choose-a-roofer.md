## The checklist

Before you agree to any roofing work, check the roofer:

- **Has public liability insurance.** Ask to see the certificate.
- **Gives a written, itemised quote**, not a figure scribbled on a card.
- **Offers a written guarantee** on the workmanship, and tells you how long it lasts.
- **Has genuine reviews** you can check, ideally from people near you.
- **Has business details you can check**, such as a company name and address.
- **Doesn't ask for most of the money up front.**
- **Will sort building regulations** if the job needs it, or is registered with a scheme like CompetentRoofer.

## Questions to ask before you agree

1. What exactly is included in the price, and what isn't?
2. Is scaffolding included?
3. What happens if you find rotten timber, and how much will that cost?
4. What materials will you use? Which brand?
5. How long will the job take, and when can you start?
6. What guarantee do I get, and is it insurance-backed?
7. Will you deal with building regulations?

A good roofer will answer all of these without fuss.

## Warning signs of a rogue trader

- **Knocking on your door** saying they "noticed some loose tiles while passing"
- Pressure to **decide today** or a "special price if you sign now"
- **Cash only**, or a large payment before any work starts
- **No written quote**, or no business details you can check
- Finding "extra problems" that suddenly need more money once they're on the roof
- Very cheap prices that seem too good to be true

If you're worried about a trader, you can report them to Trading Standards through the Citizens Advice consumer helpline.

## Your rights

If you sign a contract **in your own home**, you normally have **14 days to cancel** under the Consumer Contracts Regulations. If you want work to start within those 14 days, the trader should ask you to confirm this in writing.

Under the Consumer Rights Act, work must be carried out with **reasonable care and skill**. If it isn't, you can ask the trader to put it right.
