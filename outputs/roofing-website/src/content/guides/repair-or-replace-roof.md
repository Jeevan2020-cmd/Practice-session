## 7 signs it may be time for a new roof

1. **Leaks keep coming back in different places.** One leak is a repair. A new one every winter suggests the roof as a whole is failing.
2. **Tiles are crumbling, flaking or slipping all over the roof.** Not one or two, but across the slopes.
3. **Nail fatigue.** The nails holding the tiles rust over decades, so tiles start to slide even without storms.
4. **The felt has perished.** In the loft, felt that's brittle, torn or sagging between the rafters isn't keeping water out any more. Some older homes have no felt at all.
5. **The roof line sags or dips.** This can mean the battens or timbers have failed. Get it looked at soon.
6. **Daylight in the loft.** If you can see light through the roof, so can the rain.
7. **Age.** If the roof is likely to be original on a 1930s house, it's probably around 90 years old. Many have done well, but they don't last forever.

## When a repair is the better choice

- The problem is in **one place**: a few tiles, a ridge, a valley or the flashing
- The rest of the tiles are **sound**, and the felt and timbers are in good condition
- It's the **first problem** in years

## The cost comparison

| Option | Typical cost (3-bed semi) |
| --- | --- |
| Small repair (a few tiles) | £150 – £500 |
| Ridge re-bed or dry-fix ridge | £300 – £1,600 |
| Re-roof one slope | Roughly half to two-thirds of a full re-roof |
| Full re-roof in concrete tiles | £6,000 – £11,500 |

A useful rule of thumb from roofing price guides: if your roof needs its **third repair in five years**, get a replacement quote too, and compare.

## Ask for an honest opinion

A good roofer will tell you if a repair will do the job, even when a new roof would earn them more. Ask them to show you photos of the problem, and to explain **why** they're recommending repair or replacement.
