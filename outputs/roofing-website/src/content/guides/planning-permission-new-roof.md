There are two separate sets of rules, and people often mix them up:

- **Planning permission** is about how your house **looks** and its effect on neighbours.
- **Building regulations** are about whether the work is **safe and energy-efficient**.

## Planning permission: usually not needed

Re-roofing a house in England is generally **permitted development**. That means you don't need to apply for planning permission, as long as:

- the new roof doesn't stick out more than **150mm** from the existing roof slope, and
- it isn't higher than the highest part of the existing roof.

**Check with the council first if:**

- you live in a **flat or maisonette** (permitted development rights for houses don't apply)
- the building is **listed**
- you're in a **conservation area**. In Sutton Coldfield, that includes Four Oaks, High Street and Anchorage Road.
- you want to change the roof's **shape**, add **dormers** or significantly change its look

Bats are a protected species, and they sometimes roost in roofs. If you think you have bats, get advice before work starts.

## Building regulations: usually needed for a full re-roof

According to the Planning Portal, you can normally re-cover or repair a roof **without** a building control application only if:

- less than **50% of the roof** is affected, **and**
- less than **25% of the building's whole envelope** (walls, floor, roof, windows and doors) is affected.

You'll also normally need approval if:

- there are **structural alterations**, or
- the new covering is **15% heavier or more** than the old one, for example swapping concrete tiles for heavier ones, or
- the work changes the roof's **fire performance**.

When building regulations apply to a re-roof, you'll often need to **improve the insulation** too.

## The easy way: use a registered roofer

Roofers registered with a government-authorised **competent person scheme**, such as **CompetentRoofer**, can self-certify that their work meets building regulations. You don't need to deal with building control yourself, and you get a certificate at the end. Keep it: you'll need it when you sell your house.

If your roofer isn't registered, they or you will need to apply to Birmingham City Council's building control service (or an approved inspector) before work starts.

*This guide is general information, not legal advice. If in doubt, check with Birmingham City Council's planning and building control teams.*
